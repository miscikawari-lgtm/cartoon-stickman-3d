A town built a wall to keep out the wind. The wind took it personally.
Brindle sat on a windy hill, and every morning its washing flew off to the next county.
So the mayor ordered a wall. Ten feet high. Then twenty. Then taller than the church.
You see, wind doesn't stop at a wall. It climbs over, and drops down behind, harder than before.
The first gust took the roofs. The second took the mayor's hat.
They tore the wall down by spring. Then they bought clothes pegs.
